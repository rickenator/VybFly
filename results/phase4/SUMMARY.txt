  The uniform-radius initialization puts no degree information into the
  starting point; the degree-ranked initialization starts the hubs near the
  origin. Comparing the two at the same budget separates 'the latent geometry
  captures connectivity' from 'the initialization handed the model the degree
  ranking, which the degree-only baseline already scores well'.

Headline (fair statement of what the numbers show)

  * 2-D hyperbolic vs 3-D anatomical: AUC 0.8646 vs 0.8514 (+0.0132). The hyperbolic embedding wins.
  * mechanism check (same budget, different initialization): uniform-radius init -> AUC 0.8921, degree-ranked init -> AUC 0.8646. Both exceed the 3-D anatomical coordinates (0.8514), so the anatomical result does not hinge on the initialization. The degree-only baseline (0.8739) sits inside the spread between these two initializations, so the comparison against the degree-only baseline is unresolved by this protocol while the comparison against physical coordinates is not.
  * 2-D projection of anatomy vs 3-D anatomy: 0.8512 vs 0.8514 (2-D minus 3-D = -0.0002 AUC) - removing the dorso-ventral axis barely changes the ranking.
  * best spectral embedding (spectral_32): AUC 0.9519 / AP 0.9452 ('higher-dimensional Euclidean embeddings improve further').
  * degree-only baseline: AUC 0.8739 / AP 0.8784 (-0.4445 mean log-likelihood). spectral_32 beats it by +0.0780 AUC.
  * run-to-run spread (measured, not assumed): a second fit with the SAME configuration on a protocol variant differing by 14 neurons reaches held-out AUC 0.9012 against 0.8646 for the delivered run, on identical held-out pairs (spread 0.0366). Both runs beat the 3-D anatomical coordinates (0.8514) and the 2-D projection of them, so 'the latent geometry beats physical coordinates' is reproduced; but the margin over the degree-only baseline (0.8739) is inside this spread, so 'the latent geometry beats the degree baseline' is NOT resolved by this protocol.

Caveats that must travel with these numbers
  * the number is rank quality on held-out *pairs*, not a mechanistic claim; a geometry can win on AUC while its fitted (R, T) law is a poor generative model
  * positives and negatives are compared at a 1:1 ratio, so log-likelihood values are those of a balanced sample; the density-matched law is reported separately as R_density_matched
  * hyperbolic training budget: 150 epochs of Adam SGD over 2430462 pairs (fit on the fit edges only); see geometry.json for the full training history
  * the hyperbolic embedding is a single SGD draw: the objective is non-convex and its optimum moves with the sampled non-edges, so the delivered embedding is one sample from that distribution (run_to_run_sensitivity.json quantifies it)
  * the hyperbolic fit is initialized by total-degree rank (hubs near the origin); the budget study shows a uniform-radius initialization (no degree information) reaches a comparable optimum at the same budget, so the advantage over the anatomical coordinates does not depend on that initialization
  * the fitted (R, T) are in each geometry's own distance units (nanometres for the anatomical coordinates, eigenmap units for the spectral ones), so only within-geometry readings are meaningful: e.g. for anatomy R = 3.9e4 nm against a median positive-pair distance of 1.8e4 nm
  * adding the summed log degrees as a second feature lifts every geometry (anatomical_pca2: 0.9491, anatomical_xy: 0.9505, anatomical_xyz: 0.9492, anatomical_xyz_zscored: 0.9337, hyperbolic_2d: 0.9036, spectral_16: 0.9635, spectral_32: 0.9774); even the weakest geometry (hyperbolic_2d) then scores 0.9036 against 0.8739 for degrees alone, so all 7 geometries carry connection information that is not in the degrees
  * runtime: 1352.8s total on 20 CPUs; numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1
